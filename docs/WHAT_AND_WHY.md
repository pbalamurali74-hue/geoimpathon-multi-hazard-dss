# GEOIMPATHON 1.0 - What-and-Why Register

This register documents every dataset, software library, analytical method, and key parameter used throughout the decision-support system. It provides complete transparency and defends every design choice against common alternatives.

---

## 1. Datasets

| Item | What it is | Why we use it | Alternative we rejected and why | Where in code | Pipeline stage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sentinel-1 SAR (IW VV)** | C-band synthetic aperture radar backscatter imagery (10 m native resolution). | Cloud-penetrating day-and-night active microwave imaging; essential for detecting floodwater during cyclonic conditions (Cyclone Michaung, Dec 2023). | *Sentinel-2 optical*: Rejected because dense cloud cover during the peak cyclone obscured all optical surface signals. | `gee/extract.py:get_sentinel1_flood_extent` | Satellite Data / Validation Truth |
| **Sentinel-2 MSI** | Multispectral optical imagery (10 m / 20 m resolution). | Provides multispectral bands (Red, NIR) for computing pre-event vegetation indices (NDVI) to quantify infiltration capacity and bare soil exposure. | *Landsat 8/9*: Rejected for primary vegetation index due to coarser 30 m spatial resolution and 16-day revisit time vs. 5-day S2 constellation. | `gee/extract.py:get_sentinel2_ndvi` | Satellite Data / Hazard Modeling |
| **Copernicus GLO-30 DEM** | 30 m global digital elevation model derived from TanDEM-X radar interferometry. | Higher vertical accuracy and fewer void artifacts in coastal lowlands and urban margins compared to legacy spaceborne DEMs; provides slope, curvature, and HAND proxy. | *SRTM 30m / NASADEM*: Rejected due to documented higher vertical bias in flat coastal floodplains and vegetation-induced radar canopy bias. | `gee/extract.py:get_elevation_derivatives` | Satellite Data / Hazard Modeling |
| **CHIRPS Climatology** | Climate Hazards Center InfraRed Precipitation with Station data (0.05° ~ 5 km). | Robust 20+ year daily precipitation record; allows calculation of mean annual maximum 3-day rainfall to quantify extreme storm susceptibility. | *ERA5-Land / GPM IMERG*: ERA5 is too coarse (~9-11 km); GPM has shorter record and higher sensor drift than calibrated CHIRPS over Tamil Nadu. | `gee/extract.py:get_chirps_climatology` | Satellite Data / Hazard Modeling |
| **JRC Global Surface Water (GSW)** | Global 30 m surface water history record (1984–2021) documenting occurrence and seasonality. | Provides 38-year permanent water occurrence mask; crucial to prevent permanent lakes, wetlands, and rivers from being falsely flagged as new flood inundation. | *Static water masks (OSM / Land Cover)*: Rejected because they fail to capture seasonal waterbody dynamics and flood retention basins like Pallikaranai. | `gee/extract.py:get_permanent_water_mask` | Satellite Data / Image Processing |
| **Dynamic World V1** | Near-real-time 10 m land use and land cover (LULC) dataset produced via deep learning on Sentinel-2. | Provides pre-event composite probabilities of built-up, bare ground, vegetation, and water; built-up class serves as the exposure and impervious surface proxy. | *WorldCover / ESRI LULC*: Dynamic World provides per-pixel class probabilities rather than single hard classifications, allowing smoother runoff modeling. | `gee/extract.py:get_landcover_exposure` | Satellite Data / Hazard & Exposure Modeling |
| **OpenStreetMap (OSM)** | Community-curated vector spatial database of road networks, health facilities, and civic shelters. | Rich topological street network with road hierarchy, bridges, tunnels, and facility points needed for multimodal vehicle routing and reachability analysis. | *Google Maps / TomTom APIs*: Rejected because proprietary APIs restrict raw network graph extraction, offline edge risk weighting, and edge blocking simulations. | `routing/network.py:build_drive_graph` | Geospatial Analysis / Routing |

## 3. Analytical Methods

| Item | What it is | Why we use it | Alternative we rejected and why | Where in code | Pipeline stage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Speckle Filtering (Focal Median)** | Non-linear spatial filter replacing pixel values with the neighborhood median. | Suppresses SAR granular noise (speckle) while preserving linear water boundaries and road edges without blurring sharp water edges. | *Lee Filter / Frost Filter*: Focal median is computationally deterministic, fast to compute cloud-side in GEE, and robust against high-amplitude outliers. | `gee/extract.py:filter_speckle` | Image Processing |
| **SAR Change Detection** | Log-ratio subtraction ($\text{Post}_{\text{dB}} - \text{Pre}_{\text{dB}}$) of co-polarized backscatter. | Identifies newly inundated surfaces by detecting significant drops in radar backscatter (specular reflection away from antenna). | *Single-image thresholding*: Fails in urban environments where smooth tarmac, airport runways, and permanent calm waters mimic floodwater. | `gee/extract.py:detect_flood_change` | Image Processing / Validation |
| **Min-Max Normalization** | Linear transformation of continuous indicator values into a normalized $[0, 1]$ interval. | Standardizes heterogeneous physical units (meters, degrees, mm, indices) into a uniform dimensionless scale for multi-criteria evaluation. | *Z-score standardization*: Produces unbounded negative and positive numbers unsuitable for direct weighted sum overlays. | `analysis/mcdm.py:normalize_indicator` | Indicator / Model |
| **Fuzzy Membership Normalization** | Sigmoidal/linear membership functions assigning degrees of risk truth between 0 and 1. | Captures non-linear risk transitions (e.g. elevation risk drops sharply above 15 m rather than declining purely linearly). | *Strict step/boolean thresholds*: Discards critical gradational information and creates artificial cliff-edge boundaries in risk maps. | `analysis/mcdm.py:fuzzy_membership` | Indicator / Model |
| **Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)** | Structured Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) technique using pairwise comparison matrices. | Derives mathematically consistent criterion weights through principal eigenvector calculation, enforcing Consistency Ratio ($\text{CR} < 0.10$). | *Equal weights*: Unrealistic assumption that slope, elevation, and rainfall contribute equally to flood susceptibility. | `analysis/mcdm.py:calculate_ahp_weights` | Indicator / Model |
| **Entropy Weight Method** | Objective weighting technique based on Shannon information entropy. | Serves as an objective cross-check against subjective expert AHP weights; attributes higher weights to indicators with greater dispersion. | *Purely subjective assignment*: Entropy provides mathematical verification of indicator information content without human bias. | `analysis/mcdm.py:calculate_entropy_weights` | Indicator / Model |
| **Weighted Linear Combination (WLC)** | Multi-attribute utility overlay: $\text{Index} = \sum w_i x_i$. | Transparent, computationally efficient, peer-reviewed standard for spatial multi-hazard susceptibility modeling. | *Machine learning (Random Forest)*: Requires extensive training inventory that risks severe data leakage and produces black-box decisions. | `analysis/hazard.py:compute_weighted_overlay` | Indicator / Model |
| **ROC Curve & AUC Metric** | Plot of True Positive Rate vs. False Positive Rate across all classification thresholds. | Threshold-independent evaluation of flood susceptibility model discriminative capacity against actual Sentinel-1 flood extents. | *Single-threshold overall accuracy*: Misleading in imbalanced spatial datasets where non-flooded areas dominate 90%+ of the scene. | `analysis/validation.py:evaluate_roc` | Validation |
| **Spatial Block Cross-Check** | Spatial k-fold cross-validation grouping test samples into contiguous geographic blocks. | Prevents over-optimistic evaluation scores caused by spatial autocorrelation (Tobler's First Law) between nearby pixels. | *Random pixel sampling*: Overestimates model generalizability due to spatial auto-correlation between adjacent pixels. | `analysis/validation.py:spatial_block_cv` | Validation |
| **Dijkstra Least-Cost Routing** | Graph search algorithm finding path with minimal accumulated edge cost between nodes. | Guarantees mathematically optimal least-risk trajectory when edge cost penalizes flood hazard and impassable segments. | *A\* search*: A* requires an admissible heuristic that is difficult to formulate when edge costs are dynamically scaled by non-Euclidean risk. | `routing/router.py:find_least_risk_route` | Emergency Routing |
| **Road Removal Impact Criticality** | Systematic simulated deletion of candidate edges and measurement of newly isolated exposure and added travel delay. | Accurately discovers true single points of failure rather than abstract structural topological centrality. | *Betweenness centrality alone*: Ignores real-world hospital demand locations and the spatial distribution of vulnerable exposure. | `routing/criticality.py:compute_removal_impact` | Network Vulnerability |
| **Greedy Maximum-Coverage Pre-positioning** | Iterative facility location heuristic selecting sites that maximize covered isolated exposure. | Rapidly solves NP-hard maximal coverage location problem for relief asset staging under strict safety constraints (outside High-risk zones). | *P-median exhaustive search*: Computationally intractable for emergency laptop runtimes; greedy heuristic achieves $\ge 63\%$ theoretical optimality. | `routing/preposition.py:recommend_preposition_sites` | Decision / Recommendation |
| **Monte Carlo Sensitivity Analysis** | 200 stochastic simulations perturbing MCDM weights by $\pm 20\%$ with Dirichlet/uniform noise. | Quantifies stability of route recommendations and critical road rankings under parameter uncertainty, proving decision robustness to judges. | *Single-parameter OAT (One-At-A-Time)*: Fails to capture non-linear multi-parameter interaction effects across correlated hazard layers. | `analysis/monte_carlo.py:run_monte_carlo` | Decision / Robustness |

## 3. Key Parameters & Thresholds

| Parameter | Value | Why this value | Alternative rejected & why | Where in code | Pipeline stage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SAR Change Threshold** | `-3.0 dB` | Standard empirical threshold for backscatter reduction representing specular reflection over smooth water on rough soils. | `-1.5 dB` (too noisy, captures soil moisture), `-5.0 dB` (too conservative, misses shallow flooded vegetation). | `config.yaml:sar_parameters` | Validation Truth |
| **Safety Multiplier ($\alpha$)** | `3.0` (UI range $0.0 - 10.0$) | Balances detouring around hazardous roads against excessive travel delay. At $\alpha=3.0$, a Very High risk road ($r \approx 1.0$) incurs a 4x penalty. | $\alpha=0$ (pure distance, ignores lethal floodwater); $\alpha > 20$ (causes absurdly long 50 km detours for minor puddles). | `config.yaml:routing` | Emergency Routing |
| **Road Block Threshold** | `95th percentile` (Very High bound) | Fully blocks roads submerged in lethal deep flood water (bridges overtopping, underpass drowning hazard). | Blocking all High risk (>80th percentile) completely fragments the road graph, causing false 100% network disconnection. | `config.yaml:routing` | Emergency Routing |
| **Flood Zone Driving Speed** | `5.0 km/h` | Simulates slow, cautious movement through flooded/waterlogged streets in High-risk zones (wading vehicle speed). | Normal free-flow speed (unrealistic and dangerous) or 0 km/h (blocks passable shallow water unnecessarily). | `config.yaml:routing` | Access Analysis |
| **Golden-Hour Threshold** | `60.0 min` (and `30.0 min`) | Recognized international emergency trauma medicine standard: survival rates plummet if definitive hospital care is delayed $>60$ min. | 120 minutes: Far too lenient for acute emergency medical triage during major disaster events. | `config.yaml:access` | Access Analysis |
| **Ranking Grid Cell Size** | `500 m x 500 m` | Operates at the tactical neighborhood scale (approx 25 ha), ideal for municipal relief dispatch and sandbag staging. | 100 m (too granular, thousands of micro-cells overwhelm officers); 2 km (too coarse to distinguish local drainage boundaries). | `config.yaml:ranking_grid_size_m` | Decision Output |
| **Multi-Hazard Split** | `0.70 Flood / 0.30 Slope` | Chennai coastal corridor is predominantly flat plain with massive cyclone flood vulnerability; slope instability occurs only on localized quarry slopes and bunds. | 0.50/0.50 (grossly overweights slope hazard in a low-relief coastal delta plain). | `config.yaml:hazard_weights` | Hazard Modeling |
